Namur Invest is a lender first. The investment company of the province of Namur reports a portfolio of about 80% debt and 20% equity, for tickets of €100,000 to €2.5M in general. In four deals out of five, you give up no shares. You sign a loan, with its rate, its collateral and, often, its covenants.

I live in Gembloux, inside the province, and Namur Invest is the invest local business owners mention first. I gathered what it publishes itself (its frequently asked questions page, checked on 15.10.2026, as the site could not be re-read today), the 30-year review presented to the press in September 2025, and the 2025 annual accounts of its six companies, filed with the National Bank of Belgium. Checked on 26.11.2026. One clarification to avoid confusion: the We Invest estate agency, very present in Namur, has nothing to do with it.

## Four euros out of five are lent

That is the thesis of this article, and it changes how you prepare a file. Facing Namur Invest, the question "how many shares will I give up?" only arises in one case out of five. In the other four, the real negotiation is about the cost of the loan, its term, its ranking and the commitments that come with it.

The [Namur Invest FAQ page](https://namurinvest.be/nos-solutions-de-financement/reponsesaux-questions-courantes/) says it plainly: about 20% of the portfolio in equity, 80% in debt. It lists seven instruments. Equity stake, convertible loan, subordinated loan, unsecured loan, secured loan, bullet loan, property leasing. Only one of the seven makes you give up shares at signing. The convertible may do so later.

The same page adds a detail few invests publish: a company receives on average three to four interventions over time. Namur Invest does not finance you once. It comes back. And each return is a new negotiation, with a balance of power that depends on how the previous one went.

The group itself carries a lot of debt. The 2025 accounts of the parent company, Namur Invest SA, show a €77.8M balance sheet for €10.2M of equity and €67.6M of liabilities: a debt ratio of 87%. The summary accounts do not say where that money comes from. They do show a house that borrows in order to lend, which partly explains its preference for debt.

## How much does Namur Invest put into a deal?

The published ticket runs from €100,000 to €2.5M "in general", and the page states that it also works below €500,000. That is a range, not an average. To see where a typical file sits, you have to go back to the figures given to the press for the invest's 30th anniversary.

According to [L'Avenir on 11 September 2025](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/namur/2025/09/11/namur-invest-plus-de-5000-emplois-crees-en-30-ans-KLVPEJ6PE5B7LPONKQOULCB434/), outstanding commitments grew from €30M to €90M in twelve years, for about 200 portfolio companies and roughly €25M invested every year. The [Belga dispatch carried by La DH on 16 September 2025](https://www.dhnet.be/dernieres-depeches/2025/09/16/en-30-ans-namur-invest-a-soutenu-925-entreprises-F4JNPSP6NFABTI66WA6EU47TGM/) adds the cumulative total: €340M invested over 30 years in 925 companies.

| Calculation | Published data | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Average outstanding per portfolio company | €90M / about 200 companies (September 2025) | ≈ €450,000 |
| Cumulative amount per company supported since 1995 | €340M / 925 companies | ≈ €367,600 |
| Amount per deal, at 3 or 4 deals | €367,600 / 4 and / 3 | ≈ €92,000 to €123,000 |
| Equity share of the outstanding, if the 20/80 split applies to amounts | 20% of €90M | ≈ €18M in equity, €72M in debt |
| New commitments per year | €25M announced | €25M |

Author's calculations based on the FAQ page (checked on 15.10.2026), L'Avenir (11.09.2025) and La DH (16.09.2025). The €340M total is in nominal euros over 30 years: it understates today's amounts.

In percentage terms, the conclusion is simple. A single deal sits around €100,000, the bottom of the published range. Total exposure to one company, on the other hand, climbs towards €450,000 through successive deals. An owner asking for €2M in one go is outside the typical file. Not impossible. But expect syndication with a bank, Wallonie Entreprendre or another fund.

## Six companies behind one name

You may sign with Namur Invest SA. Or not. The company register links six companies to the same address, avenue des Champs Élysées in Namur, with a largely shared board chaired by Pierre Crevits (chair since 2013; the managing director is Renaud Hattiez).

| Namur Invest Immo Lease | 18.11.2022 | Own real estate | €0.7M | −€71,369 (−€91,408) |

Source: 2025 annual accounts filed with the National Bank of Belgium, taken from the BCE company register by [Busibee](https://busibee.be/fr/0456316803-namur-invest.md) (data published up to August 2026). Equity figures cannot be added up: cross-holdings between group companies cannot be ruled out.

Three takeaways for you. The largest pool of equity, €48.1M, sits in the venture capital company, not in the parent. A new lending company, Financement PME, doubled its equity in one year, from €4.9M to €10.9M: the invest is building up its lending capacity. And property leasing has run through a dedicated company since 2022. Ask at the first meeting which entity will sign your agreement: that is the one that will appear in your share register or your loan contract, and it does not necessarily have the same resources as its sister company.

The 2025 losses at Préface and Capital Risque are one year of accounting data, not a verdict on the quality of the portfolio. A fund that books write-downs on its holdings loses money on paper, and that is part of the job.

## Is equity gaining ground?

Here is the serious objection to my thesis. In the same L'Avenir article, management explains that it takes more equity stakes than ten years ago, when most of the money went out as loans. The accounts point the same way: €48.1M of equity in the venture capital company and €22.9M in Innovation & Growth, against €10.2M in the parent.

I take it seriously, without reversing the conclusion. The 20% equity figure is the one the invest publishes today on its own site, as checked on 15.10.2026. Even if it rises, debt stays in the majority. And the creation in 2024 of a new lending company, with €10.9M of equity at the end of 2025, shows that the invest is also strengthening its lending business.

Two nuances, however, matter for your file. The first is the convertible loan, the instrument in the middle: it starts as debt and can end up as shares, at a price set in the contract. A convertible signed today is a potential equity stake tomorrow, to be negotiated as such. The second is sectors: health, eco-construction, food and tourism are named as the most supported. In health or technology files, where the risk is higher, an equity entry is more likely than a loan.

## What do you give up when it takes equity?

A stake, a board seat, and a say over your articles of association if the stake exceeds a quarter. Namur Invest writes that it generally asks for a director's seat when it enters the capital.

The threshold that matters is set by the [Belgian Companies and Associations Code (CSA)](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/loi/2019/03/23/2019A40586/justel): changing the articles requires three quarters of the votes. A shareholder holding more than 25% can therefore block it alone. Here is the fund's stake by ticket and by your valuation before the deal.

| Ticket in shares | Pre-money €1M | Pre-money €3M | Effect on your control |
|---|---|---|---|
| €100,000 | 9.1% | 3.2% | No legal blocking power, the shareholders' agreement governs everything |
| €450,000 (average outstanding) | 31.0% | 13.0% | At €1M, it blocks any change to the articles |
| €1,000,000 | 50.0% | 25.0% | At €1M, no ordinary decision without it; at €3M, exactly 25.0%, no legal blocking power |
| €2,500,000 (published ceiling) | 71.4% | 45.5% | At €1M, it decides ordinary matters alone |

Calculation: ticket / (pre-money + ticket). A single class of shares, no multiple voting rights.

The table shows something the ticket range hides. With an average ticket, valuation decides everything: between €1M and €3M of pre-money, the same amount turns the invest from a blocking minority into a simple minority. Negotiating an extra €500,000 of valuation is sometimes worth more than negotiating a loan rate. The reserved matters listed in the agreement often weigh more than the legal threshold; our article on the [fund's veto right](/en/blog/lever-des-fonds/does-the-fund-have-a-veto) lists them.

## And when it lends, what does the loan cost?

Not published. Namur Invest gives no rate, no term, no formula. The only close public benchmark is Wallonie Entreprendre, which holds 40% of the invest: its [subordinated loan sheet from December 2025](https://cms.wallonie-entreprendre.be/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/fiche_pret_subordonne_WE.pdf) states a rate of IRS plus 1.25%, from €25,000 to €1M, over 15 years at most. Nothing says Namur Invest follows that scale.

To set orders of magnitude, take €450,000 over 7 years at 6%, an illustrative rate and not a published one. As a bullet loan, you pay €27,000 of interest a year and repay all the principal at the end: €189,000 of interest over the term. With straight-line repayment, interest drops to €108,000, but you repay about €64,300 of principal every year from year one. The bullet spares your cash flow and costs €81,000 more. A [subordinated loan](/en/blog/fonds-propres-ou-dette/subordinated-loan) has another advantage: banks often count it as quasi-equity, which can unlock a standard bank loan alongside it.

Before signing a loan offer from Namur Invest, here are the points I would have put in writing:

- the rate, fixed or floating, and its benchmark if floating;
- the ranking of the loan (subordinated or not) and what the bank asks in return;
- the covenants: ratios to respect, dividend restrictions, investments subject to approval;
- the option to repay early, and any penalty;
- for a convertible, the conversion price or formula, and who decides to convert;
- any personal guarantees asked of the owner.

Have your accountant review the whole package: they will quantify the effect on your bank ratios better than a quick read of the contract.

## Outside the province, other doors

Namur Invest's area stops at the borders of the province of Namur, north and south. If your operating site is elsewhere, the right contact is the invest of your area: Noshaq in Liège (see our [investigation into Noshaq](/en/blog/fonds-par-region/noshaq-liege-invest)), Carbon in Charleroi, Invest.BW in Walloon Brabant, IMBC in Mons. The article on [Walloon invests province by province](/en/blog/fonds-par-region/walloon-invests) gives the full map.

A Namur SME can also look for a financier active across Wallonia. ORSA (formerly Investsud), based in Marche-en-Famenne, publishes a ticket of €500,000 to €2.5M in equity or as a Smart Bond, a 5 to 10-year subordinated loan (checked on 15.10.2026 on orsafund.be). For a file above €500,000, comparing it with Namur Invest's offer is a healthy reflex, and nothing prevents the two from co-financing.

The [Walloon invests ranking](/en/classement/invests-wallons) compares what each one publishes, and the [SME fund comparison tool](/en/comparer/fonds-investissement-pme) lays tickets, instruments and areas side by side.

<Verdict>For an SME in the province of Namur, Namur Invest is the logical first meeting, especially between €100,000 and €500,000: it is the Walloon invest that publishes its range and its set of instruments most clearly. Be ready to negotiate a loan more often than an equity entry, so to discuss rate, ranking and covenants. If the offer involves shares, calculate the stake you keep on your real valuation: above 25%, the invest holds your articles of association. Have the structure checked by your accountant and your lawyer before signing.</Verdict>
