Noshaq is the invest (a Walloon provincial investment company) for the province of Liège. It publishes no ticket size, but its annual reports let you calculate one: about €720,000 per decision in 2025-2026, paid mostly as equity. It stays on the cap table for a long time, often more than ten years. In return it takes shares, a say over your articles of association and, often, a board seat.

I went through everything the group publishes itself: the group structure page, the governance page, the 2021-2026 strategic plan in its October 2023 update, the pages of its European funds, and the last two annual reviews presented to the press in September 2025 and September 2026. Checked on 06.11.2026. The goal: find out what a Liège business owner gives up when Noshaq comes in.

## One name, sixteen funds

Noshaq is not a fund. It is a group, formerly Meusinvest, managing sixteen in-house and delegated vehicles according to its strategic plan. When you submit a file, you do not necessarily know which entity will sign the shareholders' agreement.

The [group structure page](https://noshaq.be/en/about-us/structure-of-the-group/) lists around fifteen companies, plus three partner non-profits. The table keeps the ones that could one day end up on your cap table, and adds Noshaq Europe 4, which has its own page.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Noshaq Europe 4 | SMEs under 250 FTE, set up in July 2024 | Fixed-rate loans or equity | Province of Liège | Close to €38M ERDF 2021-2027 |

Not one row shows a ticket. That is the first finding, and it is awkward for an owner who wants to know whether he is knocking on the right door before writing a forty-page file.

The second is about the entity. A stake taken by Noshaq Europe 4 follows EU state aid rules: an SME under the EU definition, not in difficulty, excluded sectors, a viable business plan. A stake taken by Noshaq SA has none of these constraints. Ask at the first meeting which company will sign, because the answer changes the eligibility conditions and, sometimes, the duration.

## How much does Noshaq put on the table?

About €720,000 per decision over the last financial year. That figure is published nowhere: I calculated it from the amounts and the number of decisions the group announces every year.

| Period | Decisions | Amount approved | Average ticket | Equity share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-2026 (full plan) | 647 | €596.1M | €921,000 (calculated) | Not published |
| 2024-2025 | 104 | €81.6M | €785,000 (published) | €64.2M, i.e. 78.7% |
| 2025-2026 | 115 | €82.8M | €720,000 (calculated) | Close to 70% |

Sources: [2024-2025 review, Aktus CCI, 11.09.2025](https://aktus-cci.be/2025/09/11/le-groupe-noshaq-cloture-une-annee-historique/) and [2021-2026 plan review, Aktus CCI, 09.09.2026](https://aktus-cci.be/2026/09/09/noshaq-boucle-son-plan-2021-2026-avec-596-millions-deuros-dinvestissements/).

An average hides a lot. The September 2026 review states that real estate, industry and energy carry the largest tickets, above €1M. A services SME looking for €200,000 is therefore not out of scope: it simply pulls the average down. And the strategic plan sets €50,000 as the threshold above which the board must approve, which suggests much smaller amounts also go through.

Energy deserves a line. Amounts approved there rose from €5.1M in 2024-2025 to €16.4M in 2025-2026, more than three times as much in a year. If your project involves energy production or storage in the province of Liège, you are in the sector Noshaq is building up right now.

## 70% in shares: the stake you give up

Noshaq lends, but it invests in equity first. Almost four euros out of five in 2024-2025. That is where your stake is decided.

Worked example built for this article. The average ticket of €720,000, entirely in new shares, on three pre-deal valuations.

| Valuation before the round (pre-money) | Stake taken by Noshaq | Stake you keep | Effect on your control |
|---|---|---|---|
| €1.5M | 32.4% | 67.6% | The fund is above 25%: on its own it can block any change to the articles |
| €3M | 19.4% | 80.6% | No statutory blocking minority, everything goes through the agreement |
| €5M | 12.6% | 87.4% | A supporting stake, the board seat is still to be negotiated |

In percentage terms: between a €1.5M and a €3M pre-money, the same cheque costs 13 points of equity. And it pushes the fund above the 25% threshold. The Belgian Code of Companies and Associations (CSA) requires three quarters of the votes to amend the articles of an SRL (private limited company) as well as an SA (public limited company): above 25%, the fund can veto any change to the articles, without needing a clause. Some decisions, such as changing the corporate purpose of an SA, even require four fifths.

That is why I look at the valuation and the 25% threshold before the amount. An owner who takes €720,000 on €1.5M "because that's the ticket" has just handed over a statutory veto. He may accept it. But knowingly.

The loan portion (€17.2M in 2024-2025) does not dilute. The Noshaq Europe funds advertise fixed-rate loans, with no published rate or term. When a [shareholders' agreement](/en/blog/lever-des-fonds/shareholders-agreement) is negotiated alongside a loan, read the two documents together: a loan can carry covenants that weigh as much as a board seat.

## Who decides on your file?

Three levels. The investment manager builds the file. The investment committee shortlists it: four people, CEO Gaëtan Servais, the CFO, the COO and the chief legal officer. The board of directors, fifteen members chaired by Michaël Vanloubbeeck, takes the decision.

The strategic plan sets the threshold: above €50,000, the board approves. In practice, almost every SME file goes all the way up.

This process has two consequences. The first is timing: a board decision follows the board's meeting calendar, not your cash needs. Ask for the date of the next board meeting as soon as the committee has approved. The second is the group's own shareholding: since 17 July 2023, Nethys has held 22.39% of Noshaq after a €39.8M capital increase, and sits on the board. Nothing unusual, but your investor has public and semi-public shareholders of its own, and its priorities follow the province's strategy.

The 2026-2031 plan, announced in September 2026, widens the scope: artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, defence, space and aeronautics join life sciences, energy, industry 4.0, agri-food, the experience industry and real estate. In 2025-2026, these priority ecosystems received 98% of the amounts approved.

Two percent for everything else. If your business fits none of these boxes, say so plainly at the first meeting and ask whether the file stands a chance.

## Liège first, Verviers and Huy-Waremme next

The portfolio leans towards the city. The strategic plan admits it: 70% of holdings are in the Liège district, 20% in Verviers and 10% in Huy-Waremme, while those areas weigh 60, 25 and 15% in population and businesses. Noshaq has set itself the goal of rebalancing.

For an owner in Herve, Spa or Waremme, that is an argument to put on the table, figures in hand. The invest has written itself that it wants more files from your area.

In the east, one detail. The German-speaking municipalities and their neighbours fall under Move Invest, managed by Noshaq, and Move Immo, a joint subsidiary with Ostbelgieninvest. An SME in Eupen or Sankt Vith therefore has two doors, and it helps to know which one leads to which vehicle.

Outside the province, the door closes. The Noshaq Europe funds require an operating site in the province of Liège, or a commitment to open one. For an SME in Namur or Charleroi, see our article on the [Walloon invests province by province](/en/blog/fonds-par-region/walloon-invests) or the [Walloon invests ranking](/en/classement/invests-wallons), which compares what each one publishes.

## How long does Noshaq stay on the cap table?

A long time. Noshaq publishes no holding period, and it is the question that matters most to know when you will be able to sell or buy back.

I did the simplest possible calculation. In 2025-2026, the portfolio held 480 companies, with 35 entries and 41 exits. In 2024-2025, 478 companies and 31 exits. Dividing the stock by the flow of exits gives an average presence of 12 to 15 years. It is an order of magnitude, not a promise: exits also include bankruptcies and repaid loans, and an average mixes a biotech sold after five years with an industrial SME backed for twenty.

The message is still clear. Noshaq is not a closed-end fund forced to sell within seven years. The September 2026 review speaks of "patient investment, rooted in its territory", and the figures bear it out: 1,133 companies backed since 1985, an average dividend of 4% a year paid to its own shareholders over the plan.

Patient does not mean no exit. The 2021-2026 plan calls for faster exits and requires an "exit" chapter in every investment memo. In other words, the exit question is raised internally from day one. It should be on your side too, in the agreement: buy-back price, timing, pre-emption right. Our guide on [buying back the fund's shares](/en/blog/lever-des-fonds/buying-back-the-funds-shares) sets out the three possible routes.

The Odoo exit, in 2024-2025, shows the other side. Noshaq sold most of its stake when CapitalG, Sequoia and BlackRock came in, keeping a token holding. When a buyer turns up at the right price, the invest sells.

## When to look elsewhere

Noshaq covers many cases in the province of Liège. Not all of them. Here are the situations where I would look elsewhere first, or alongside:

- You refuse any dilution. Equity makes up close to 70% of the amounts approved. A subordinated loan from a private fund can do the same job on your balance sheet. ORSA (formerly Investsud), active across Wallonia since 1985, publishes a ticket of €500,000 to €2.5M and a Smart Bond, a 5 to 10-year subordinated loan whose return is partly linked to EBITDA (checked on 15.10.2026 on orsafund.be). See also our article on the [subordinated loan](/en/blog/fonds-propres-ou-dette/subordinated-loan).
- Your head office is in Brussels. Noshaq will not step in without a Liège base. finance&invest.brussels advertises up to €5M per SME, as a co-investor.
- You are an ULB spin-off, not an ULiège one. Theodorus invests from €250,000 in the first round, and up to €2.5M per company (checked on 04.11.2026).
- You want an exit date known in advance. A closed-end fund imposes one, Noshaq does not. Depending on your personal horizon, that is an advantage or a drawback, and our article on [evergreen and closed-end funds](/en/blog/comprendre-les-fonds/evergreen-vs-closed-end-fund) frames the choice.
- Your need exceeds what a single player can carry. [Wallonie Entreprendre](https://www.wallonie-entreprendre.be/fr/a-propos/), the regional financing body, invests in equity, loans and guarantees, and often co-invests with the invests.

To compare the tickets, instruments and areas each player publishes, the [SME fund comparison tool](/en/comparer/fonds-investissement-pme) puts them side by side, and the [ranking of investment funds for SMEs](/en/classement/fonds-investissement-pme) scores them.

<Verdict>For an SME in the province of Liège, Noshaq is almost always the first meeting to book: it has the volume (€82.8M approved in the last financial year), the patience (more than ten years of average presence) and the range. Its weakness is the opacity of its ticket and loan terms, which you will only learn by negotiating. Before accepting an equity offer, have your accountant calculate the stake you keep and check with your lawyer whether the fund crosses the 25% threshold: that is where your freedom to amend your articles is decided.</Verdict>
