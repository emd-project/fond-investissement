In Brussels, the public financing body is finance&invest.brussels. It lends, guarantees and takes equity stakes of up to €5 million for SMEs, but always alongside a bank, a fund or other co-investors. Its subsidiary brusoc looks after the self-employed and very small businesses. The rest of the round is yours to find.

## The thesis: in Brussels, the public body complements, it does not fund alone

A Walloon business owner has two public layers above them: Wallonie Entreprendre (the Walloon Region's public financing body) and their province's invest. A Brussels owner has only one. And that single layer defines itself as a complement.

Everything else follows from this. [finance&invest.brussels](https://finance.brussels/) says it plainly: it invests alongside banks, investment funds and other co-investors. Its €5m ceiling for SMEs is a co-investment ceiling. It does not say "we bring €5m"; it says "we can go that far if others come in".

I have seen this misunderstanding backfire on owners. They turn up at the regional body with their whole need and no bank or investor on the other side, believing the public body is the lender of last resort. It is the opposite. The public body comes last in the order of the deal, not as a last resort.

## Who funds SMEs in Brussels?

One regional public body, a subsidiary for small files, the banks, and a handful of private funds whose area covers Brussels. The list is shorter than people think.

I took the nine players in my 15.10.2026 survey, the ones in the [ranking of funds for SMEs](/en/classement/fonds-investissement-pme), and looked at a single column: the area they publish. The question: does an SME whose entire activity is in Brussels fall within their scope, unconditionally?

| Player | Published area | 100% Brussels SME automatically covered? |
|---|---|---|
| finance&invest.brussels | Brussels-Capital Region | Yes |
| Theodorus | Belgium, with an opening to Canada (Theodorus IV) | Yes, at seed stage |
| ORSA (formerly Investsud) | Wallonia | No, unless it has significant activity in Wallonia |
| Wallonie Entreprendre | Wallonia | No |
| Noshaq | Province of Liège | No |
| Sambrinvest | Charleroi Métropole | No |
| Namur Invest | Province of Namur | No |
| Invest.BW | Walloon Brabant | No |
| IMBC | Mons, Borinage and Centre | No |

Two out of nine. In percentage terms, that is 22% of the players in this survey.

This table does not say the Brussels market is empty. It says my survey is built around Wallonia, and that most French-speaking public and semi-public financiers are Walloon. For a Brussels SME, that means less choice in the public sphere and a heavier reliance on banks and private funds.

Theodorus, the other open player, is a seed fund linked to the ULB (Université libre de Bruxelles). It publishes €250,000 for a first investment, up to €2.5m (Theodorus IV: €500,000 to €2.5m), in seed and early-stage equity, for spin-offs and deep tech start-ups. Its funds II and III are closed-end over 12 to 14 years, fund I is evergreen, and it reports over €70m under management. It does not fund a distribution SME or a workshop looking to expand. For the owner of a conventional SME, that often leaves one public player, plus the banks.

## What does finance&invest.brussels actually offer?

Three families of solutions, depending on company size. Checked on its website on 15.10.2026.

| Audience | Published solutions |
|---|---|
| Self-employed and very small businesses | Loans, guarantees, proxi loan (through brusoc) |
| Start-ups and scale-ups | Amounts and forms of financing designed to complement banks, funds and co-investors |
| Mature SMEs | Loans, guarantees and equity stakes up to €5m, as a co-investor |

It also invests indirectly, by subscribing to venture capital funds. That is a way of backing private investors who will, in turn, fund Brussels businesses.

The guarantee is the most underrated tool on the list. When I analysed business loans in Namur, a public guarantee was not an administrative detail: it changed the share of risk the bank carried alone, and therefore the decision. A file turned down at 100% bank risk can go through when part of it is guaranteed. The mechanism is the same in Brussels. The guarantee rates and coverage of finance&invest.brussels are not in my survey: not published here, ask for them.

### brusoc and the proxi loan, for the smallest files

brusoc is the subsidiary of finance&invest.brussels for the self-employed and very small businesses. The proxi loan, offered through brusoc, draws on private individuals' savings, often people close to the owner, to fund a small Brussels business. The amounts and the benefit granted to the lender are not in my survey: check them with brusoc before you ask family and friends.

## The objection: a private fund would be simpler

The objection is serious. Why go through a public body that requires co-investors, when a private fund could bring the full ticket?

Because published tickets have a ceiling. Apart from finance&invest.brussels, the players in my survey that publish a ticket cap out at €2.5m (ORSA, Theodorus, Namur Invest, Invest.BW). And Invest.BW writes that it never funds the whole of a need on its own: sharing risk is the stated rule, not the exception.

There is also a cost argument. A private fund taking equity is paid in a percentage of your company. A public loan or guarantee takes no share. A made-up example: your SME is worth €2m before the round and needs €1m.

- All equity from a fund: it holds 1 out of €3m post-money, i.e. 33%.
- €500,000 of guaranteed bank credit plus €500,000 of equity: the fund holds 0.5 out of €2.5m, i.e. 20%.
- Same structure, but only €250,000 of equity and €250,000 of regional loan: the fund holds 0.25 out of €2.25m, i.e. 11%.

From 33% to 11%. The public layer does not replace the fund, but it reduces what you give up to it. That is the real reason to build a round with several parties, well before the question of who to call.

## The nuance: when private money takes the lead

The three-layer structure is not always the right one. Three cases where a private fund legitimately takes the central role:

- the company is a deep tech start-up or a spin-off, and the natural first financier is a seed fund such as Theodorus;
- the need exceeds what a bank will lend, because the company is still losing money;
- the owner wants a shareholder who brings a network or a structured board, not just money.

In these cases, finance&invest.brussels remains useful as a co-investor, but it follows rather than leads.

I have not checked the published terms of other private funds likely to invest in Brussels, such as Gimv or the venture capital funds backed by the regional body. So I do not give you their tickets: not published in this survey.

## Can a Walloon fund finance you from Brussels?

Yes, if you have real activity in Wallonia. No, otherwise.

The invests are limited to their province. Some Walloon private funds accept a company that develops significant activity in Wallonia: [ORSA](https://orsafund.be/booster-ma-scale-up/), for example, targets companies based in Wallonia or developing significant activity there. A Brussels SME opening a production site in Walloon Brabant can therefore fall within the scope of Invest.BW or ORSA, for that site.

The edge case I come across: a registered office in Brussels and a mere business address in Wavre. That is not enough. Activity, jobs and production are what count. The full map of who to call is in [funds and public financing by region](/en/blog/fonds-par-region/funds-and-public-financing-by-region).

## How do you build a round in Brussels?

A typical round for a Brussels SME combines a bank loan, a regional guarantee or loan, and money from a private fund or private investors. The order matters.

1. Work out the total need and how much the bank can cover with its collateral.
2. Present finance&invest.brussels with a round in which it complements that bank or a private fund, not one in which it carries everything.
3. Compare the regional offer with a private fund's on horizon and instruments.
4. Calculate, for each scenario, the stake you keep after the round.

For a small business, the [start-up tax shelter](/en/blog/start-up-et-scale-up/tax-shelter-start-ups-scale-ups) can make investment by private individuals more attractive. It can be combined with the proxi loan within the rules of each scheme, which your accountant should check.

<Warning>Do not approach a Walloon invest for a business with no activity in Wallonia, and do not expect the regional body to fund a large project alone. Both refusals are predictable.</Warning>

The final decision depends on your file, your accounts and your articles of association. Have the structure reviewed by an accountant or a corporate lawyer before you sign a letter of intent.
