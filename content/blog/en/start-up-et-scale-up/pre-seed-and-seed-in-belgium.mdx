A Belgian pre-seed usually sits between a few tens of thousands of euros and €250,000. It pays for proof that a market exists. The share you give up depends on two figures: the valuation, and the form of the money (shares, convertible loan or plain loan). At 20% per round, you keep 64% after the seed.

## What is pre-seed, and where does seed begin?

Pre-seed pays for validation. Seed pays for going to market. The line is not drawn by the amount, it is drawn by the proof you bring to the next round.

| Round | Purpose | Proof expected at the end | Frequent funders in Belgium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | Prototype, customer interviews, first hires | A verified need, customers ready to pay | Founders, family and friends, Coup de pouce loan, Startup Boost, business angels |
| Seed | Product on the market, first paying customers | Recurring revenue, a measured acquisition cost | Business angels, seed funds (Theodorus), invests |
| Series A | Sales team, replication | Growth that repeats | Venture capital funds, ORSA, invests, private funds |

The full picture of funding rounds is in our article on [funding a start-up or a scale-up](/en/blog/start-up-et-scale-up/funding-start-ups-scale-ups-biotech). Here, I stay with the first outside money.

## How much should you raise at pre-seed?

The amount that takes you to the seed milestone, plus six months.

I start from spending, never from a figure heard at a conference. A built example: two founders on minimum pay, one developer, a prototype to have made, €12,000 a month. Over 18 months, that comes to €216,000. Rounded up, the round is €250,000. If the milestone needs 12 months, raising for 12 months means going back to investors the day the bank account is empty, and negotiating the seed under pressure.

An oversized pre-seed costs too. Every euro raised before the proof is paid for at the lowest valuation the company will ever have.

## How much equity do you give up at pre-seed?

In shares, 10 to 20% is the range to aim for. Above 25%, the seed becomes hard to close.

The calculation never changes: the investor's share equals the amount raised divided by the valuation after the round. €250,000 raised on a €1M pre-money valuation gives €1.25M post-money, so 20% for the investor.

Dilution multiplies from one round to the next. With 20% at pre-seed and 20% at seed, founders keep 80% × 80% = 64%. With 30% at pre-seed, they keep 70% × 80% = 56%, and Series A is still to come. Eight points of capital lost in the first round never come back. Carta, which tracks the cap tables of thousands of start-ups, says it bluntly in an analysis dated 19 September 2025: [selling 30% at pre-seed is far above the market median](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-preseed-dilution-30-percent-too-much/).

## Who funds a pre-seed in Wallonia and Brussels?

Few players publish a ticket that fits. Here are those that do, with their instrument and area as they publish them.

| Player or scheme | Published ticket | Instrument | Area | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wallonie Entreprendre, Startup Boost | Up to €100,000 | Convertible loan | Wallonia, start-ups under 3 years old | 01/11/2026 |
| Coup de pouce loan | €75,000 per lender, €300,000 per company | Loan from private individuals, 2.5% yearly tax credit for the lender | Wallonia | 01/11/2026 |
| Start-up tax shelter | 45% or 30% tax reduction, €100,000 per investor per year, €500,000 per company | Share subscription by private individuals | Belgium (federal) | 01/11/2026 |
| finance&invest.brussels | €100,000 to €5,000,000 | Convertible loan, equity or subordinated loan | Brussels | 01/11/2026 |
| Theodorus | €250,000 as a first investment | Equity | Belgium, ULB spin-offs | 15/10/2026 |

Sources: the [Startup Boost page of Wallonie Entreprendre](https://startup.wallonie-entreprendre.be/en/startup-boost/), the [Walloon Government press release of 27 August 2026](https://jeholet.wallonie.be/files/Communiqu%c3%a9s%20de%20presse/20260827_cp-coup-de-pouce.pdf) on the Coup de pouce loan, the [FPS Finance](https://fin.belgium.be/fr/particuliers/avantages-fiscaux/investir-start-up-scale-up-tax-shelter) (SPF Finances, the federal tax administration) and the [finance&invest.brussels product brochure](https://finance.brussels/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Brochure-produits-web-fr.pdf).

Two readings. In Brussels, the public ticket starts at €100,000: below that, it is family, friends and business angels. And ORSA, the site's partner, sits above pre-seed because of its ticket size; it becomes relevant at the next round, when the need exceeds €500,000. In Flanders, PMV has its own tools, outside the scope of this site.

Selection is real. In the first Startup Boost call, 92 applications were filed and 8 were selected, according to the [press release of 17 June 2026](https://jeholet.wallonie.be/files/Communiqu%c3%a9s%20de%20presse/20260617_cp-startupboost.pdf).

## Convertible loan or shares: what are you really giving up?

A convertible loan pushes the price question to the next round. It does not remove it, and two clauses decide the bill: the discount and the valuation cap.

A built example. You receive €100,000 as a convertible, the size of a Startup Boost. A year later, you raise €1M at seed on a €4M pre-money valuation (converted loan included). With a 20% discount alone, the lender converts at 80% of the seed price: they get 2.5% of the capital, and you keep 77.5%. With a cap set at €1M, they convert at the cap price, much lower: 7.3% for them, 72.7% for you. Same money, same round, a 4.8-point gap.

Had the same €100,000 come in as shares on a €500,000 post-money valuation, it would have taken 20% from day one. The convertible saves you equity only if the seed happens at a good price.

Startup Boost conversion terms are described as "standardised" but are not detailed on the public page: not published. Ask for the terms document before applying. Have these put in writing:

- the discount applied to the next round's price, as a percentage;
- the valuation cap, if any, and whether it is pre-money or post-money;
- the maturity, and what happens if no round takes place before it: repayment, forced conversion or extension;
- the minimum round size that triggers conversion.

A liquidation preference may also come with the shares received on conversion. Its effect on a sale price is set out in our article on [liquidation preference](/en/blog/comprendre-les-fonds/liquidation-preference).

## Does the Coup de pouce loan dilute you?

No. It is a loan, made by a private individual close to you to your Walloon company, who remains a creditor.

The reform adopted on 27 August 2026 changed the figures. The lender's tax credit becomes 2.5% a year for the whole term of the loan, against 4% for the first four years before. The cap per lender falls from €125,000 to €75,000. The cap per company rises from €250,000 to €300,000. Four relatives at €75,000 each therefore cover the pre-seed in the example, without selling a single share. For each of them, in percentage terms, that is €1,875 of tax credit a year.

The downside: it has to be repaid. Before market proof, it is a bet on your ability to repay people close to you.

## Does the tax shelter make a business angel cheaper?

For them, yes. For you, not directly. A business angel who subscribes €25,000 of shares in a micro-company under 4 years old gets 45%, or €11,250, back on their income tax. Their net outlay falls to €13,750. The share of capital they receive is still the one set by the valuation. Caps and conditions are covered in our article on the [start-up and scale-up tax shelter](/en/blog/start-up-et-scale-up/tax-shelter-start-ups-scale-ups).

## When should you move to seed?

When the pre-seed milestone is reached and at least six months of cash remain.
