No, not by default. Belgian law gives a minority shareholder no general veto. A fund gets one in three ways: by going above 25% of the votes, by having a list of decisions reserved to it in the shareholders' agreement, or by holding a class of shares of its own. All three are negotiated before signing, almost never after.

## Does the fund get a veto as soon as it joins the capital?

No. A shareholder votes at the general meeting in proportion to its shares. Most decisions there are taken by simple majority: approving the accounts, allocating the result, appointing or removing a director. With 20% of the votes, the fund loses those votes if it stands alone against you.

The veto comes from elsewhere. It comes from the [Code of Companies and Associations](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/loi/2019/03/23/2019A40586/justel) (CSA, Belgium's company code) when the fund crosses a threshold, and from the contract when you grant it. The second source often weighs more than the first.

## From what percentage can a minority shareholder block?

Above 25% of the votes. An amendment to the articles requires three quarters of the votes cast, with a quorum of half the shares at the first meeting (art. 5:100 for the SRL, 7:153 for the SA, the Belgian public limited company). Abstentions do not count.

| Share of votes | Minority shareholder's rights | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 share | Attend the meeting, ask questions, pre-emption right on new shares issued for cash | CSA |
| 10% of shares (SRL); 1% of votes or €1,250,000 of capital (SA) | Have an expert appointed to examine the books and transactions; same thresholds for a minority action in court | Art. 5:106 and 7:160 (expert) |
| 10% of shares (SRL) | Require the meeting to be convened within three weeks | Art. 5:83 |
| More than 25% | Block any amendment to the articles: share issues, mergers, dissolution | Art. 5:100 and 7:153 |
| 30% | Ask a court to exclude a shareholder for just cause | Art. 2:63 et seq. |
| More than 50% | Decide ordinary matters alone | CSA |
| 75% | Decide everything, articles included | CSA |

Sources: CSA, [overview of shareholder rights](https://www.lexgo.be/fr/actualites-et-articles/14641-quels-sont-vos-droits-en-tant-qu-actionnaire-un-apercu-pratique-du-droit-de-vote-du-controle-et-de-la-protection-des-minoritaires) published by the law firm NOMA on LexGO in February 2026, [list of mandatory provisions](https://www.ibr-ire.be/docs/default-source/fr/Documents/actualites/nouveau-CSA/Liste-exemplative-dispositions-imperatives-CSA.pdf) from the Institute of Company Auditors (IRE).

The point owners discover too late concerns the SRL. Since 2019 it has had no share capital, but the number of shares is written into the articles. Issuing new shares therefore amends the articles. A fund at 26% can block every later round, including the one you would need to dilute it.

## €500,000 invested: what percentage, what power?

It all depends on the pre-money valuation. I took €500,000, the minimum ticket published by ORSA (checked on orsafund.be on 15.10.2026), and four valuations.

| Pre-money valuation | Fund's stake | Legal veto over the articles? |
|---|---|---|
| €1,000,000 | 33.3% | Yes, alone |
| €1,400,000 | 26.3% | Yes, alone |
| €2,000,000 | 20.0% | No, unless with an ally |
| €4,500,000 | 10.0% | No |

In percentages: below a €1.5M valuation, €500,000 takes the fund above 25%. At exactly €1.5M, it holds exactly 25.0%, and your 75% is still enough.

Arguing over €100,000 of valuation, here, means arguing over the veto.

<Warning>The threshold is read on votes cast, not on the fund's stake alone. A fund at 22% voting with a business angel at 8% gathers 30% against you: the amendment fails. If the angel abstains, your 70% makes 76.1% of the votes cast, and it passes.</Warning>

## Which decisions does the fund want a veto over?

The ones that put its money at stake. The list arrives in the letter of intent, as reserved matters or qualified majority. The ones I see most often:

- the annual budget and the investment plan;
- any borrowing above a set amount;
- investments or asset disposals above a threshold;
- hiring, pay and departure of managers;
- dividend distributions;
- setting up subsidiaries, entering a new business;
- issuing shares, warrants or a stock option plan.

Each line can be defended. The problem comes from the total and from the thresholds. When I read the agreement of an SME in Hainaut, from the management controller's seat, the investment threshold requiring the invest's consent was lower than the plant's annual maintenance budget. Replacing a press required a meeting. Nobody had put a figure on it at signing.

## A class of shares of its own, and the fund votes separately

This is the least visible veto. Changing the rights of a class of shares requires a majority within each class (see the [types of SRL shares](https://blog.oeccbb.be/fr/article/types-dactions-de-la-srl-et-droits-y-relatifs/29401) set out by the OECCBB, the Belgian order of accountants). If the fund alone holds the "A" shares, it has a veto over any change affecting them, even at 10%. That is often the case when it obtains a [liquidation preference](/en/blog/comprendre-les-fonds/liquidation-preference).

## Can the fund's veto last forever?

No, if it runs through a voting agreement. Agreements on the exercise of voting rights must be limited in time and cannot run against the company's interest; otherwise they are void (art. 5:46 for the SRL, 7:56 for the SA, as the [OECCBB](https://blog.oeccbb.be/fr/article/les-conventions-dactionnaires-et-conventions-de-vote-rappel-bien-utile./24600) reminds). The 25% threshold, on the other hand, never lapses: it lasts as long as the fund holds its shares.

## How do you limit the veto before signing?

| Case | What to do |
|---|---|
| The fund would go above 25% | Defend the valuation, or replace part of the equity with a [subordinated loan](/en/blog/fonds-propres-ou-dette/subordinated-loan), which carries no vote |
| Open-ended list of reserved matters ("including") | Insist on a closed list |
| Euro thresholds without indexation | Set them on the actual budget, with annual indexation |
| Veto over the annual budget | Provide that, failing agreement, the previous budget stays in force |
| Veto with no end | Tie it to a minimum stake held by the fund, and to its presence in the capital |
| Persistent deadlock | Write a procedure: mediation, then exit at a price set by an expert |

The legal thresholds are the same in Wallonia, Brussels and Flanders: the CSA is federal. The investor, though, varies. Namur Invest says it generally asks for a director's seat when it takes equity, Invest.BW always invests alongside other funders, and finance&invest.brussels takes minority stakes (checked on 15.10.2026, detailed in our article on the [Walloon invests](/en/blog/fonds-par-region/walloon-invests)). ORSA held stakes in 69 companies at the end of 2025, for €55M invested (La Libre, 19.06.2026). None of the four publishes its list of reserved matters: not published.

<Tip>Ask for the list of reserved matters at the letter of intent stage, with figures on every threshold. Once due diligence has started, each change is negotiated against the valuation.</Tip>

<Verdict>A minority fund almost always has a veto, but rarely the one the law gives it. The 25% threshold is defended through the valuation; the list of reserved matters is defended line by line, figures in hand. The [shareholders' agreement](/en/blog/lever-des-fonds/shareholders-agreement) is signed with your lawyer, not the fund's.
