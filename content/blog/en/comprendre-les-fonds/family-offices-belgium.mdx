A family office can be an excellent shareholder for an SME: no end date, and families that have often run a business themselves. But for a Walloon or Brussels SME looking for less than €3m, it is rarely the right door. There are few of them in Wallonia, and those that publish a ticket start above that.

## What is a family office in Belgium?

Two different trades share the same name. That alone causes many wasted meetings.

A **single family office** manages the wealth of one family and, in some cases, invests directly in unlisted companies. Korys for the Colruyt family, SPDG for the Périer D'Ieteren family: these can become your shareholders. A **multi-family office** advises several families on their wealth. It structures a succession, selects investments, coordinates lawyers. It does not join your share register.

The orders of magnitude are known. In May 2025 Forbes Belgium counted more than 680 Belgian families with wealth above €25m, about 150 family offices and some €40bn under supervision. The [Belgian Family Office Association](https://www.forbes.be/fr/le-secteur-belge-du-family-office-se-structure-et-se-professionnalise/), founded by intuitae, Family Partners, Yours Advisory and the BNP Paribas Fortis Family Office, groups multi-family offices. They are advisers.

Belgium has no legal status for family offices. A family investing its own money needs no licence and has no duty to publish its terms. So any comparison with a fund starts from a half-empty page.

## How many family offices invest from Wallonia?

Five. The [Belgian Family Office Monitor](https://openthebox.com/en/resources/blog/family-office-monitor) published by openthebox on 19 March 2026 screened the annual accounts of thousands of Belgian holding companies and kept 99, with €43.3bn of equity. I grouped its figures by region.

| Region | Family offices identified | Equity | Share of total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flanders (5 provinces) | 78 | €26.1bn | 60.3% |
| Brussels | 16 | €13.6bn | 31.4% |
| Wallonia | 5 | €3.6bn | 8.3% |
| of which Hainaut | 1 | €2.7bn | 6.2% |
| of which Liège province | 3 | €779m | 1.8% |
| of which Walloon Brabant | 1 | €155m | 0.4% |
| Namur, Luxembourg | 0 | 0 | 0% |
| **Belgium** | **99** | **€43.3bn** | **100%** |

Source: openthebox, Belgian Family Office Monitor 2026 (annual accounts up to 2024). Regional grouping and percentages calculated on 17.11.2026.

Headquarters are not everything. A Ghent family office can invest in Charleroi, and most invest well beyond their province. But the network matters. A family office rarely comes through a web form: it hears about you from its banker, its lawyer or a business owner it knows. In Namur or Arlon, that network is thin.

## Family office, closed-end fund, evergreen fund: the comparison

The table sets the three shareholders an SME actually meets against each other. Terms come from each player's published pages, checked on 17.11.2026 unless stated otherwise.

| Criterion | Direct-investing family office | Closed-end fund | Evergreen fund or invest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Published example | Korys (Colruyt family), Sofina (Boël family among shareholders) | Theodorus II and III | ORSA, provincial invests |
| Published ticket | Korys: €3m to €30m; Sofina: €20m to €100m (growth), €100m to €300m (long-term minority); SPDG: not published | Theodorus: €250,000 in the first round, up to €2.5m (checked 04.11.2026) | ORSA: €500,000 to €2.5m |
| End date | None | 12 to 14 years | None |
| Target stake | Korys: minority or majority; Sofina: minority | Usually minority | ORSA: not published |
| Board seat | Sofina: usually sought | Not published | Not published |
| Published terms | Rarely | Yes, often | Yes |
| Access | Network, corporate finance adviser | Application, calls | Application, meeting |

Sources: [Korys Direct Investments profile](https://vlaio.be/nl/begeleiding-advies/financiering/risicokapitaal/korys-direct-investments) at VLAIO (updated 07.02.2025), [Sofina's long-term minority investments page](https://www.sofinagroup.com/investments/our-investment-styles/long-term-minority-investments/), [ORSA's private equity page](https://orsafund.be/expertises/private-equity-belgique/) (updated 06.10.2026).

The ticket row settles it. For its consumer division, Korys also requires revenue above €10m and a profitable company. Sofina invests worldwide, India and the United States included. Both look at companies that the vast majority of Walloon SMEs are not.

## Who stays longest on the share register?

On paper, the family office. Korys states it has no end date. Sofina presents its capital as permanent, which lets it extend a holding and follow on in later rounds. A closed-end fund must sell everything before its term: 12 to 14 years for Theodorus II and III. A 12-year fund that invests in your company in its sixth year has only six years left. The detail is in the page on [evergreen and closed-end funds](/en/blog/comprendre-les-fonds/evergreen-vs-closed-end-fund).

In practice, I would add a caveat. A family office's horizon is written nowhere. It depends on a family, its generations, its liquidity needs and its quarrels. None of the three family offices cited publishes an average holding period. Nor does ORSA, for that matter.

A contractual end date has one merit: you know it on signing day. With a family office, you know an intention. Have it turned into clauses.

## A €3m minimum ticket: the stake you give up

A large ticket is not a problem in itself. It becomes one when it exceeds the real need, because every extra euro is paid for in equity. Here is the share of the company handed over for the investment, depending on your SME's valuation before the investor comes in.

| Pre-money valuation | ORSA, €1m | ORSA, €2.5m (published cap) | Korys, €3m (published floor) | Korys, €10m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| €4m | 20.0% | 38.5% | 42.9% | 71.4% |
| €8m | 11.1% | 23.8% | 27.3% | 55.6% |
| €15m | 6.3% | 14.3% | 16.7% | 40.0% |

Calculation: investment ÷ (pre-money valuation + investment). It says nothing about the terms actually offered, which are negotiated case by case.

On an SME valued at €4m, the Korys floor takes the investor above 25% of the votes. At that threshold it can block any change to the articles of association on its own, and in an SRL (Belgian private limited company) every issue of new shares. The page on [the fund's veto](/en/blog/lever-des-fonds/does-the-fund-have-a-veto) explains what that changes.

<Warning>Do not inflate your need to reach a family office's minimum ticket. Raising €3m when €1.2m is enough means giving up more than twice the equity for cash that will sit idle on the balance sheet.</Warning>

## Board seats and club deals: the rights requested

A family office rarely invests alone. According to PwC's study of family office deals, [reported by MandA](https://manda.be/articles/how-family-offices-are-driving-investment-growth-in-belgium/) in March 2025, 84% of Belgian family office investments in 2024 were made alongside other investors. Private equity made up 54% of their investments that year.

For you, this means two things. The family office often arrives with a fund, or behind one, and the agreement will be negotiated by several parties. And the rights it asks for look like a fund's: Sofina says it usually seeks board representation in its growth investments. Korys also offers shareholder loans, convertible or not.

## How do you approach a family office without a directory?

Through the network, almost always. There is no front desk. Before the first meeting, check five points:

- does the family office invest directly in companies, or only in funds and listed securities;
- does its published ticket, or failing that the size of its last announced deals, match your real need;
- does it target a minority or a majority stake, and with which voting rights;
- who decides on its side: a family member, a committee, a salaried manager;
- how did it exit its last holdings: buy-back by the owner, resale, sale to a trade buyer.

To make contact, your corporate finance adviser or your auditor often knows the families active in your sector. A fund already talking to you may also bring in a family office as co-investor. VLAIO's risk capital database also holds profiles of family investors with their ticket, such as Korys.

If your need is below €3m, I would start elsewhere: with evergreen funds and the [Walloon invests](/en/blog/fonds-par-region/walloon-invests), which publish their terms and answer an application. The public versus private question is covered in [public invest or private fund](/en/blog/comprendre-les-fonds/public-invest-vs-private-fund).

<Verdict>A family office suits a profitable SME with more than €10m of revenue, looking for at least €3m and a shareholder with no end date. Below that, which is the case for most Walloon and Brussels SMEs, an evergreen fund such as ORSA (€500,000 to €2.5m) or a provincial invest meets the same need for a long horizon, with published terms. The tickets of nine players are in the [ranking of investment funds for SMEs](/en/classement/fonds-investissement-pme), and the [comparison tool](/en/comparer/fonds-investissement-pme) puts them side by side. Either way, have your lawyer review the letter of intent before you sign.</Verdict>
