A placement fund collects savings from individuals and invests them, mostly, in listed shares and bonds. An investment fund, in the sense an SME owner means it, provides equity or subordinated loans to unlisted companies for several years. Same word, two businesses, two different readers.

## What is the difference between a placement fund and an investment fund?

The difference fits on one line: the first buys securities anyone can buy, the second finances companies nobody can buy on a stock exchange.

A placement fund comes in forms every bank customer has seen on a statement: SICAV, UCI (undertaking for collective investment, "OPC" in French), ETF, fund of funds. In Belgium, major banks such as Belfius and KBC distribute them to retail customers. The saver buys units and can usually sell them back any business day.

An investment fund on the company side works at the other end. Its backers are institutions, entrepreneurs, public bodies. It takes a stake in an SME, a start-up or a company being bought out, or lends to it on a subordinated basis. It only sells after several years, and often to a single buyer.

| | Placement fund | Investment fund (SMEs) |
|---|---|---|
| Who provides the money | Savers, often individuals | Institutions, entrepreneurs, public bodies |
| Where the money goes | Mostly listed shares and bonds | Unlisted companies: SMEs, start-ups, buyouts |
| Common forms | SICAV, UCI, ETF, fund of funds | Private equity funds, venture capital, invests |
| Selling units | Often daily | Rare, after several years |
| Reader of this site | No | Yes: owner, founder, buyer |

## The word "fund" costs both readers

My thesis is simple: the confusion is not just a vocabulary problem. It wastes the time of both people typing the same word, and sometimes costs one of them money.

The saver looking for "investment fund" lands on pages about pre-money valuations, exit clauses and €500,000 tickets. They close the tab, or worse, conclude that this world is open to them through a simple bank transfer. The business owner, for their part, lands on comparisons of SICAV management fees and bank product sheets. They leave without learning that the invest in their province publishes a ticket size.

When I worked in the business lending department of a bank in Namur, between 2009 and 2017, I saw owners arrive with printed return tables from placement funds, convinced that "the fund" would expect the same annual return from them. The conversation had to start from scratch. A fund that finances an SME does not think in terms of a displayed annual return: it thinks in terms of an exit price, years later.

Here are the figures that separate the two worlds, as SME funds publish them themselves (survey of 15.10.2026):

| Published indicator | SME funds surveyed | What it says about liquidity |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum published ticket | €100,000 (Namur Invest), €250,000 (Theodorus), €500,000 (ORSA) | The fund invests in a company, not in a savings portfolio |
| Investment horizon | 5 to 10 years (Invest.BW) | The money is tied up for years |
| Subordinated loan term | 5 to 10 years (ORSA's Smart Bond) | Repaid after all other debts |
| Life of a closed-end fund | 12 to 14 years (Theodorus II and III) | Exit driven by the fund's term, not by the saver |
| Selling units | Rare, after several years | The opposite of a SICAV unit you can sell any day |

Every row of this table is a reason for a saver not to put their deposit account into it.

## Does a placement fund finance SMEs?

Indirectly, and almost never yours. A placement fund that buys listed shares finances listed companies, and even then it mostly buys existing shares from other investors, with no fresh money reaching the company.

The serious objection deserves a hearing. Some placement funds target small and mid-caps, and the word "SME" sometimes appears in their marketing name. A saver can therefore believe, in good faith, that they are financing the local economy.

Here is the nuance: a small cap listed on a stock exchange is still a listed company. A 40-person Walloon SME that is not listed has no shares to sell on a market. For a fund to come in, you need a capital increase, a shareholders' agreement and months of negotiation. No retail placement fund does that work; it is neither its job nor its legal framework.

There are routes for individuals who really want to finance SMEs, but they lie elsewhere. Some SME funds accept private investors, on terms they set themselves. And the tax shelter allows direct investment.

## The tax shelter: the individual's way in

The [start-up and scale-up tax shelter](https://fin.belgium.be/fr/particuliers/avantages-fiscaux/investir-start-up-scale-up-tax-shelter), described by the SPF Finances (the Belgian Federal Public Service Finance), gives an individual a tax reduction of 25% to 45% depending on the company they invest in, capped at €100,000 a year. It is the tax tool that links private savings directly to young Belgian companies.

The risk, however, remains whole. The tax reduction cushions part of a possible loss; it does not remove it. And the money stays locked in an unlisted company, with no market to sell into.

On the company side, eligibility conditions are covered in the page on the [start-up and scale-up tax shelter](/en/blog/start-up-et-scale-up/tax-shelter-start-ups-scale-ups). I deal with them there, from the point of view of an owner who wants to raise money from individuals.

Regional differences matter little here: the tax shelter is federal, and it applies in Namur as it does in Brussels or Ghent. Public investment bodies, by contrast, are regional: Wallonie Entreprendre in Wallonia, finance&invest.brussels in Brussels, PMV in Flanders. None of the three is a placement fund open to savers.

## You want to invest your savings: where to look

This site does not compare placement funds and gives no investment advice. I am not the right person to ask, and I would rather say so in two lines than fill a page. For neutral information:

- [Wikifin](https://www.wikifin.be/fr), the FSMA's financial education programme, explains how funds work, their fees and their risks.
- [The FSMA](https://www.fsma.be/fr) (Financial Services and Markets Authority, the Belgian regulator) publishes the list of authorised intermediaries and warnings about firms offering investments without authorisation.
- Your bank (Belfius, KBC or another) distributes placement funds and must assess your profile before offering you one.
- An authorised independent intermediary can compare products from several providers, provided their authorisation appears on the FSMA website.

<Warning>An offer that promises a high return with no risk, or pressures you to decide quickly, should set off alarms. Check the firm on the FSMA website before transferring anything. FSMA warnings often target firms that borrow the vocabulary of investment funds to look credible.</Warning>

Then look at who operates near you. In Wallonia, provincial invests (Noshaq, Sambrinvest, Namur Invest, Invest.BW, IMBC) and private funds such as ORSA publish tickets from €100,000 to €2.5M. In Brussels, finance&invest.brussels states up to €5M for an SME, as co-investment. ## Why is the word "fund" so confusing?

Because the law and everyday language draw the line in different places. In supervisory terms, the FSMA distinguishes collective investment undertakings offered to the public from managers of alternative investment funds. Private equity and venture capital belong to the second family. For a saver, however, "investment fund" means anything their bank sells under that name.

Marketing adds another layer. A savings product is happily called an "investment fund" because the word sounds serious, and a venture capital fund calls itself a "fund" because that is its legal form. Both are right about the word. They are not talking about the same thing.

I draw a reading rule from this, valid for both audiences:

- if you hear about units, ongoing charges and a daily price, it is a placement fund;
- if you hear about tickets, a shareholders' agreement and an exit price, it is an investment fund in the SME sense;
- if someone promises you both at once, check their authorisation with the FSMA before going any further.
