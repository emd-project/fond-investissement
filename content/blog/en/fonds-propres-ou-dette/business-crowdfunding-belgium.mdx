A Belgian SME can raise up to €5,000,000 over twelve months from private individuals, through a platform authorised by the FSMA. There are three forms: a loan, a bond or shares. The first two give up no share of the capital; on a 7% bond they cost 8% to 10% a year once the fee is included. Shares are paid for in percentage points.

I spent eight years analysing business credit applications in Namur. Seen from there, a crowdfunding campaign is a loan like any other, with one difference: your lenders are three hundred private individuals, and some of them are your customers.

## Loan, bond or shares: what do you actually give up?

Nothing in capital, if you borrow. A share of the capital, if you issue shares. Everything else follows from that choice.

| Form | The investor receives | You give up | You owe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loan (crowdlending) | A claim on the company | No shares, no board seat | Interest and principal, on fixed dates |
| Subordinated bond | A debt security, ranking behind the bank | No shares | Fixed interest; at Bolero, 1 to 5 years |
| Shares (equity) | A share of the capital, often through a vehicle | A percentage, and what it is worth at exit | Nothing due; an exit expected one day |

Debt dominates in Belgium. The FSMA study covering July 2018 to June 2020 counts 70% of campaigns as loans, for 85% of the amounts sought; successful campaigns raised €494,574 on average, €246,925 at the median.

With shares, one detail changes your cap table. At Spreds, investors do not become your direct shareholders: they hold notes issued by Spreds Finance, which opens one compartment per company and remains your only legal shareholder. You manage one line in the register, not three hundred. The voting rights of that vehicle are not described on the page checked on 14.11.2026: ask for the text before signing.

## Which authorised platforms for a Belgian SME?

EU Regulation 2020/1503 has applied since November 2021. In Belgium, the FSMA authorises platforms and publishes their list, along with the platforms from other member states that have notified their activity here ([Wikifin, page updated on 14.09.2026](https://www.wikifin.be/fr/epargner-et-investir/produits-dinvestissement/le-crowdfunding)). Check yours is on it before any meeting.

| Platform | Published instrument | Published amount for the company | Published cost for the company | Source and date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bolero Crowdfunding (KBC) | Bonds, 1 to 5 years, 5% to 10% gross | Up to €5M per company | 5% to 7% success fee | Bolero brochure 2023 |
| Spreds | Shares through Spreds Finance; SME loans announced for 2026 | €150,000 minimum in shares, €100,000 in loans | Not published for the company | spreds.com, checked on 14.11.2026 |
| Look&Fin | Loans, mostly real estate according to La Libre | Not published | Not published | European licence announced in June 2023; La Libre, 17.04.2026 |
| WinWinner | SME loans | Not published | Not published | La Libre, 17.04.2026 |
| Ecco Nova | Loans, mostly real estate according to La Libre | Not published | Not published | La Libre, 17.04.2026 |

Two readings. Bolero is the only player publishing its fees for the company; the others answer case by case. And Spreds, which so far financed start-ups (70 successful campaigns in 2025, about €170,000 per start-up according to La Libre of 17.04.2026), announces SME lending with no date and no terms. Spreds also appears in our [ranking of business angel networks](/en/classement/reseaux-business-angels).

## How much does a €300,000 campaign cost?

Take a Walloon SME raising €300,000 through a five-year bond at 7%, a rate in the middle of the range published by Bolero. The success fee runs from 5% to 7%.

| Item | Repaid in one go (bullet) | Constant principal, €60,000 a year |
|---|---|---|
| Success fee (5% to 7%) | €15,000 to €21,000 | €15,000 to €21,000 |
| Net amount received | €279,000 to €285,000 | €279,000 to €285,000 |
| Interest over five years | €105,000 | €63,000 |
| Effective annual cost | 8.3% to 8.8% | 9.0% to 9.9% |
| Share of capital given up | 0% | 0% |

Constant repayments cost less interest but more per year: the fee, paid upfront, is spread over a balance that shrinks every year. In percentage terms, that makes the money clearly more expensive than a bank loan, and clearly cheaper than a stake in the capital if the company succeeds.

With shares, the maths is different. €300,000 on a €1.5M valuation before the round costs you 16.7% of the capital. On €3M, 9.1%. No interest, no maturity; but if you sell one day for €10M, that 9.1% is worth €910,000.

## The seven steps, from first meeting to payout

The timeline below follows the process Bolero describes in its June 2020 guide for entrepreneurs and the deadlines in the EU regulation. Other platforms follow a similar pattern.

1. Choose the form. Debt if you refuse to give up capital and your cash flow covers the repayments; shares if the company cannot repay yet. One hour with your accountant is enough to decide.
2. Check eligibility. At Bolero in 2020: registered office in Belgium, SRL, SA or cooperative, at least two years of activity, positive equity. At Spreds: first commercial results in Europe.
3. Build the file. Annual accounts, growth plan, business plan. Bolero's selection committee gives its opinion within 48 hours.
4. Write the key investment information sheet. You write it yourself, six A4 pages at most, in an official language of the state that authorised the platform or a language its authority accepts (Article 23 of the regulation).
5. Set the minimum and the target amount. Below the minimum, the campaign fails and everyone gets their money back.
6. Launch the campaign. Allow four to six weeks between the first meeting and the launch at Bolero. A non-sophisticated investor can withdraw within four calendar days of their offer (Article 22).
7. Collect, then report. The funds arrive a few days after closing, net of the fee. The platform gives you a register and a payment file; the repayments are your job for the whole term.

## Which mistakes sink a campaign?

Platforms publish little about their rejections. The points below come from the conditions they display and from what a credit analyst looks for in a file.

- Negative equity: Bolero rules it out from the start.
- A minimum set too high. The whole campaign fails, and the file deposit (up to €5,000 at Bolero in 2021) does not come back.
- A key information sheet that promises more than the business plan. It engages your liability.
- Forgetting the bank. A non-subordinated loan can clash with a clause in your existing credit; a subordinated bond, on the contrary, reassures it.
- Raising equity without a reviewed shareholders' agreement. The platform's vehicle becomes a shareholder, and your next round will be negotiated with it too.

The last point is the one I see most underestimated. An article on notaire.be noted as early as 2014 that a crowd of small shareholders can get in the way of a later capital increase. The single vehicle softens the problem. It does not remove it.

## Wallonia, Brussels, Flanders: loans from your circle with a tax credit

If the money comes from people you know, a platform is not always needed. Each Region has its own private-to-business loan, with a tax credit for the lender.

| Region | Scheme | Cap per lender | Cap per company | Lender's benefit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wallonia | Coup de pouce loan | €75,000 | €300,000 | 2.5% tax credit a year (reform of 27.08.2026) |
| Brussels | Proxi loan | €50,000 a year, €200,000 in total | €250,000 | 4% for the first three years, then 2.5% |
| Flanders | Winwinlening | €75,000 | €300,000 | 2.5% a year, at most €1,875 |

Sources: [Walloon Government press release of 27 August 2026](https://jeholet.wallonie.be/files/Communiqu%c3%a9s%20de%20presse/20260827_cp-coup-de-pouce.pdf), [Wikifin, page updated on 23.01.2026](https://www.wikifin.be/fr/impots-emploi-et-revenus/declaration-dimpots/vos-investissements-avantages/vous-pretez-un) for the proxi loan, [VLAIO (Flemish business agency), page amended on 31.07.2026](https://www.vlaio.be/nl/subsidies-financiering/subsidiedatabank/winwinlening) for the Winwinlening. In Flanders, the agreed rate must sit between half the legal interest rate and the full legal rate, i.e. 2.25% to 4.5% in 2026. On €300,000 at 4.5% for five years, that is €67,500 in interest, with no platform fee. The lender and the company must be in the same Region.

The FSMA's 2020 study also shows these loans run through platforms: loan campaigns averaged 6.02% without them, 4.17% with them included.

## Crowdfunding or a fund: at what amount should you change doors?

Beyond €500,000 to €1M, the question arises. La Libre puts SME crowdlending between €500,000 and €1M, and equity crowdfunding usually around €250,000. Above that, the €5M legal ceiling remains theoretical for an SME.

At those amounts, a fund comes in, with a ticket and clauses. ORSA, the site's partner, publishes a ticket of €500,000 to €2.5M in equity and quasi-equity for companies in Wallonia, including a Smart Bond, a 5 to 10-year subordinated loan whose return partly depends on EBITDA (checked on 15.10.2026 on orsafund.be). A fund's [subordinated loan](/en/blog/fonds-propres-ou-dette/subordinated-loan) and a crowdfunding bond play the same role on the balance sheet, with one creditor instead of three hundred. The [SME fund comparison tool](/en/comparer/fonds-investissement-pme) sets published terms side by side, and our article on [quasi-equity](/en/blog/fonds-propres-ou-dette/equity-debt-and-quasi-equity) explains how the bank counts each one.

<Verdict>Below €500,000, with cash flow that covers the repayments, a subordinated crowdfunding bond costs 8% to 10% a year and takes none of your shares: it is expensive, but nothing follows you to the exit. If your circle is ready to lend, the regional loan with a tax credit is cheaper. Shares through a platform make sense when the company cannot repay yet, provided you have read the vehicle's rights. Have your accountant model the scenario, and your lawyer review the key information sheet, before you sign it.</Verdict>

<Tip>Private investors subscribing to your new shares may claim the [start-up or scale-up tax shelter](/en/blog/start-up-et-scale-up/tax-shelter-start-ups-scale-ups), which often helps the campaign. The text of each campaign must state it explicitly.</Tip>
