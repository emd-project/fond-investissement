A vendor loan and an earn-out both defer part of the price of your SME. The difference fits in one line: with a vendor loan, the price is fixed and you wait to be repaid; with an earn-out, the amount depends on future results. In the first case you become the buyer's lender. In the second, you bet on figures you no longer run.

## Vendor loan, earn-out: two ways of waiting for your money

A vendor loan is credit that the seller grants to the buyer. The price is set at signing, say €3M. The buyer pays part of it at closing and owes you the rest, on a schedule and, normally, with interest. That balance is a firm debt: it does not move with results.

An earn-out is a price top-up. It is only due if the company hits targets written into the share purchase agreement, almost always EBITDA or revenue over two or three financial years. It may be worth the full amount, part of it, or nothing.

The first solves a funding problem: the buyer does not have all the money. The second solves a disagreement on price: you believe in the business plan, the buyer less so. Mixing them up means accepting a risk that does not match your problem.

## How many Walloon sellers agree to wait?

One in four. The [2025 report by WE Cession & Acquisition](https://cms.wallonie-entreprendre.be/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Reporting-WE-Cession__Acquisition-2025.pdf), the Wallonie Entreprendre team that took over from Sowaccess, covers 544 business transfers handled by its accredited partners.

The same report fills in the rest of the picture. The average multiple is 5.6 times EBITDA (across 319 transactions with data), 41% of deals are below €1M and 79% below €5M. Excluding buyouts funded 100% with equity, the buyer's own contribution averages 27%: the remaining 73% comes from the bank, Wallonie Entreprendre, a fund, or you. Earn-outs are on the rise (14% of deals in 2024, 17% in 2025), while the weight of vendor loans is slipping slightly (25% of the package in 2024, 23% in 2025).

## The comparison, criterion by criterion

| Criterion | Vendor loan | Earn-out |
|---|---|---|
| Problem solved | The buyer lacks funding | You and the buyer see different prices |
| Deferred amount | Fixed, known at signing | Variable, from zero to the cap |
| Average share in Wallonia (2025) | 23% of the financing | 21% of the price |
| Your main risk | Holding company default, ranking behind the bank | Missed results, or results restated by the buyer |
| Usual term | Aligned with the bank loan, often several years | Two to three financial years |
| Return | Negotiated interest | None, unless late-payment interest is agreed |
| What to negotiate first | Subordination, security, early repayment triggers | Definition of the metric, audit right, acceleration |
| Tax since 2026 | Gain at sale, interest at 30% withholding (in principle) | Gain on the top-up once it becomes certain (per the parliamentary record) |

## On an SME sold for €3M, how much do you actually receive?

I take a company in the €1M to €5M bracket, where 38% of Walloon transfers sit, and the report's average proportions. Share price: €3M. Vendor loan: 23%, i.e. €690,000, repaid in five annual instalments of €138,000 at 4% (rate chosen for the example). Earn-out: 21%, i.e. up to €630,000, paid if cumulative EBITDA over the first two years hits the target, nothing below 80% of target, pro rata in between.

| Scenario | €690,000 vendor loan | €630,000 earn-out |
|---|---|---|
| Received at closing | €2,310,000 (77%) | €2,370,000 (79%) |
| Target met | €3,000,000 + €82,800 gross interest | €3,000,000 |
| EBITDA at 90% of target | €3,000,000 + €82,800 (the debt is still owed) | €2,685,000 |
| EBITDA at 80% or less | €3,000,000 + €82,800 (the debt is still owed) | €2,370,000 |
| The holding defaults after two years | €2,635,680 received, €414,000 still owed, behind the bank | Top-up exposed to the same default |

As a percentage: at 90% of target, the earn-out costs you 10.5% of the price. The vendor loan costs you nothing as long as the holding pays, and earns you €57,960 in net interest after 30% withholding tax.

Its risk sits in the last row. An acquisition holding repays its bank, and you, out of dividends from the company it bought. If the target loses a major client in year 3, the bank gets paid first.

## What does a vendor loan cost you?

Your ranking. The buyer's bank almost always requires your loan to be subordinated to its own: you are paid only if its instalments are met and its ratios respected, and in a bankruptcy you come after it. It works like a [subordinated loan](/en/blog/fonds-propres-ou-dette/subordinated-loan), except that this time the lender is you, with no monitoring team and no credit committee. You also lose any way to get your shares back: they belong to the holding from closing, and the bank usually takes the first-ranking pledge over them.

Before signing, these are the points I have written down in black and white:

- the exact repayment schedule, with the share the bank allows to be paid each year, not just "after the bank loan is repaid";
- an interest rate, even a modest one, and a late-payment rate;
- immediate repayment of the balance if the buyer resells the company, merges it or stops paying your instalments;
- a second-ranking pledge over the target's shares or a personal guarantee from the buyer, knowing that the bank will have to agree to it;
- an information right: annual accounts and a quarterly statement for as long as you are a creditor.

## What does an earn-out cost you?

Control of the metric. On closing day, you no longer decide on investments, on management fees charged by the holding, or on accounting methods. A buyer who hires two sales reps in year 1 lowers the EBITDA of the very year your top-up is calculated on, without cheating. Hence the need to lock into the agreement the definition of the metric, the restatements allowed, your right to have the accounts checked, and recourse to an expert or arbitrator if you disagree.

An earn-out still has a real advantage over a low price. If your order book doubles in the year after the sale, you share in it. For a seller who stays on for two years as managing director, it is often the fairest structure.

## Does the tax authority treat both the same way?

Not quite, and 2026 changed the picture. The law on capital gains on financial assets, published in the Belgian Official Gazette (Moniteur belge) on 21 April 2026, applies from 1 January 2026. If you alone hold at least 20% of the company, the gain is exempt up to €1M per five-year period, then taxed at 1.25% up to €2.5M, 2.5% up to €5M, 5% up to €10M and 10% above that. Below 20%, the rate is 10% after an annual exemption of €10,000. For shares held before 2026, the starting base is a value at 31 December 2025, which can notably be set at equity plus four times EBITDA, or established by a registered auditor.

Example: €3M price, €1.4M reference value. Gain of €1.6M, of which €1M is exempt. Tax: €600,000 × 1.25% = €7,500.

For earn-outs, a [note published by the Belgian Institute of Registered Auditors (IRE) on 10 March 2026](https://www.ibr-ire.be/fr/reglementation-et-publications/doctrine/notes-techniques/note-technique-detail-page/traitement-d-une-clause-de-earn-out-par-le-futur-imp-t-sur-les-plus-values-sur-les-actifs-financiers) reports the Finance Minister's position: a top-up linked to a sale signed before 2026 escapes the new tax. For a sale signed since, the gain on the top-up is taxed once it becomes certain. One question remains open: should the initial price and the top-up be added together to apply the scale? The administrative circular and your tax adviser will settle it.

<Warning>Selling to your children's holding company with a vendor loan? If you control the buyer, alone or with your spouse and relatives up to the second degree, the law calls it an internal capital gain, taxed at 33% with no exemption. In principle, a family transfer through a holding falls into that box. Have the structure checked by a tax lawyer before you sign anything.</Warning>

## Wallonia, Brussels, Flanders: same contract, different public lenders

Vendor loans and earn-outs fall under contract law and federal tax. The rules are the same in Namur, Brussels and Ghent.

The public lenders differ. In Wallonia, the [Wallonie Entreprendre subordinated loan](https://cms.wallonie-entreprendre.be/wp/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/fiche_pret_subordonne_WE.pdf) ranges from €25,000 to €1M per project, at the IRS rate plus 1.25%, over the term of the accompanying bank loan, fifteen years at most (fact sheet checked on 10.11.2026). In 2025, the institution granted 272 subordinated loans and 294 guarantees on business transfers, and guaranteed 59% of the bank loan on average. Every euro it puts in is a euro you do not have to lend. The [transfer voucher](https://www.wallonie.be/fr/demarches/etre-accompagne-par-un-expert-pour-ceder-ou-reprendre-une-entreprise-cheque-transmission) (chèque transmission) covers 75% of an accredited expert's fees, up to €15,000 excluding VAT over three years: enough to have the earn-out clause reviewed.

On the fund side, ORSA publishes a ticket of €500,000 to €2.5M in equity for buyouts of Walloon SMEs with 20 to 150 staff (checked on 15.10.2026 on orsafund.be). The Walloon invests took part in 23 business transfers in 2025, for €946,000 on average. In Brussels, finance&invest.brussels lends, guarantees or takes a stake of up to €5M per Brussels SME, always as a co-investor (checked on 15.10.2026); our guide to [financing a business in Brussels](/en/blog/fonds-par-region/financing-a-business-in-brussels) covers the other tools. A fund at the table shrinks your vendor loan, but it also joins the [buyout negotiation](/en/blog/reprise-et-transmission/buying-or-selling-an-sme-with-a-fund), and the [ranking of funds for SMEs](/en/classement/fonds-investissement-pme) compares those that publish their terms.

<Verdict>If your buyer is short of money, accept a vendor loan, but a limited one, with interest and an early repayment trigger: it is a firm debt, and your only real risk is the strength of the holding. If you disagree on price, the earn-out is the tool, provided you stay in the business or lock down the definition of the metric. Either way, the deferred amount should not exceed what you can lose without changing your retirement plans. Your accountant models the default scenario, your lawyer drafts the clauses.</Verdict>
